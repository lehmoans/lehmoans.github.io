01 CFD of Blood in an Artificial Heart Monash Heart Hack · Jan 2024 – Dec 2025 +

Using CFD to simulate and derive characteristics of blood in an artificial heart.

01A

Rotary Heart Pump · 2024

Work

Conducted CFD analysis of heart designs submitted to the 2024 Heart Hack Competition. The team was invited to present its results at the grand final in Japan and won “Best Business Plan”.

The simulation work produced varied results and highlighted the need for further research and verification of CFD techniques.

General ANSYS Fluent method

  • Meshing: fluid region divided into a fine rotational region near the pump blades and a mid-to-coarse stationary region near the pump walls.
  • Material: blood approximated using a power-law model.
  • Model: steady-state flow approximated using k-ω SST.
  • Motion: angular velocities applied at the rotational-region walls.
Key limitation The steady moving-rotor formulation does not holistically represent the cardiac performance of a mechanical heart.

Further modelling interests

  • Blood rheology, particularly at low Reynolds numbers.
  • Greater understanding of the steady-state model rather than treating it as a black box.
  • Transient modelling for more realistic mechanical-heart performance.
  • Numerical approximation models relevant to CFD verification.

Validation challenge

Large discrepancies were observed between simulation data and experimental results. Model assumptions and inaccuracies are therefore a major area for further refinement.

01B

Pulsatile Heart Pump · 2025

Work

Researched and conducted Fluid Structure Interaction (FSI) simulations using ANSYS Workbench and successfully simulated small-deformation coupled systems.

Technical challenge

The team underestimated the complexity of FSI because the fluid and structural domains require different simulation techniques. I was responsible for the design of the simulation method.

Large-deformation limitation The required design deformation could not be simulated reliably. The FEA component produced errors when the material needed to elastically deform up to approximately 80%.

Investigated causes

  • Hyperelastic material limitations.
  • Incorrect or unsuitable mesh settings.

Approaches investigated

  • UDF-based methods, with significant implementation complexity.
  • Approximating the 3D membrane as a surface, which introduced additional meshing problems.
